Every idea below is built from things QuestLab does today: scenes to explore, characters to question, objects to collect, questions with feedback, gates that open when a student has earned them, glossary words and a checklist. Each shows:

- **The hook**: why students will care,
- **What they do** in the world,
- **Questions worth asking**, in the mission and in the room,
- **Your move**, the teacher's part,
- and a **prompt** to paste into the [generator](/docs/editor/plans-and-generator/#the-ai-mission-generator), or notes on building it by hand.

## Science

### 1. The Wilting Stream (grades 6 to 8)

- **Hook:** The plants beside a stream are dying and nobody knows why.
- **What they do:** Collect samples from three sites, test the pH, look under a microscope, report the source.
- **Questions:** *Why collect a healthy plant too? What does a pH of 3 tell you? What evidence separates an infection from a poison?*
- **Your move:** Pause midway for the pH scale. Debrief with first answers.
- **Build it:** It ships with QuestLab as part of the *Starship Explorer* sample. Duplicate it and change the setting.

### 2. Follow a Raindrop (grades 4 to 5)

- **Hook:** You are a raindrop. Where will you end up?
- **What they do:** Travel through a mountain valley, pass through evaporation, condensation and precipitation, and fix a broken weather station by answering a question about each stage.
- **Questions:** *What happened to the water here? What made it change? Where does it go next?*
- **Your move:** Have teams draw the cycle from memory afterwards and compare it with the one in the mission.
- **Prompt:**

  ```text
  A mission for grade 5 about the water cycle. Students follow a raindrop through a
  mountain valley, find evaporation, condensation and precipitation, and fix a broken
  weather station by answering questions about each stage.
  ```

### 3. The Rainforest Research Station (grades 3 to 4)

- **Hook:** Plants at a jungle research station are failing and the scientists are away.
- **What they do:** Test light, water and air in different corners of the station, compare results, work out what plants need to grow.
- **Questions:** *What did you change? What stayed the same? How do you know it was the light?* (a first lesson in fair tests)
- **Your move:** Ask teams to explain *how they made their test fair*.
- **Prompt:**

  ```text
  A mystery in a rainforest research station: students work out what plants need to
  grow (light, water, air). Grade 4.
  ```

### 4. The Space Market (grades 5 to 7)

- **Hook:** A trader will sell you what the crew needs, if you can work out the best deal.
- **What they do:** Compare prices per unit across several stalls, scale a recipe for the crew, and type their answers in answer boxes that the mission checks.
- **Questions:** *Which is the better buy, and how do you know? What do you do to both amounts to keep the ratio the same?*
- **Your move:** The reasoning is the lesson. Ask students to defend their choice to a team that chose differently.
- **Build it:** An **answer box** saves what the student types in a variable; a condition on a variable can open a gate or a reward. The [infobox page](/docs/editor/infoboxes/#answer-boxes) shows how. Or use the generator with the prompt *"Ratios at a space market, grade 6"*.

## English language arts

### 5. Who Took the Trophy? (grades 5 to 8)

- **Hook:** The school trophy is missing. Four people were seen near the case.
- **What they do:** Interview four characters (each [conversation](/docs/editor/conversations/) is a branching tree), collect clues and notice who contradicts whom.
- **Questions:** *Which statement is supported by two sources? Whose story changed? What is the difference between what a character saw and what they assumed?*
- **Your move:** Make students cite the *line* that supports their conclusion. It is a lesson in using evidence from a text, with the text being the interviews.
- **Build it:** Great for a hand-built mission: one scene, four characters, one conversation each, a notebook objective list, and a final question where the student names the culprit and one piece of evidence.

## History and civics

### 6. Market Day in Ancient Rome (grades 6 to 8)

- **Hook:** Spend a day in a Roman market, and notice what people can and cannot buy.
- **What they do:** Talk to a merchant, a slave, a senator's wife and a soldier. Each describes the same event differently. Students collect what they hear in a notebook.
- **Questions:** *Whose voice is missing? Which source would you trust, and for what? What would the soldier not tell you?*
- **Your move:** Put different perspectives side by side at the debrief. Ask students what a historian would need to check.
- **Build it:** Conversations with **Offered only when** conditions let a character say more once you have earned their trust.

### 7. The Town Hall Meeting (grades 7 to 10)

- **Hook:** A town must decide whether to close the old swimming pool to build a library.
- **What they do:** Visit residents with different stakes and listen to them. Collect strongest points from each, then complete a statement of what the town should do, with reasons.
- **Questions:** *Who gains and who loses? What evidence would settle the factual disagreements? Which values are in tension?*
- **Your move:** Keep the debrief even-handed. The aim is to practise weighing evidence and understanding positions, not to arrive at the "right" decision.

### 8. A Café in Madrid (any language, beginners)

- **Hook:** You have just arrived, and you are hungry.
- **What they do:** Order from a waiter, ask for the bill, find the toilets, and talk to a stranger. Each exchange is a branching conversation with choices in the target language.
- **Questions:** *Which reply is polite? What does this word mean? What would you say if you did not understand?*
- **Your move:** Give a role-play task afterwards: students act out the same conversation without a screen.
- **Build it:** Write the dialogue as [conversations](/docs/editor/conversations/); a line can have a **recording** attached if you want students to hear it. Glossary words give the vocabulary, and the report counts them.

## Life and learning skills

### 9. The Group Project (grades 6 to 10)

- **Hook:** Your group has a presentation due, and one member is not pulling their weight.
- **What they do:** Talk through the situation with each character, choose what to say, and see how the choices change the team.
- **Questions:** *What did you say, and what did it do? What else could you have said? How did each person feel?*
- **Your move:** This works best as a discussion starter. Let students try different paths, then compare them.

### 10. Spot the Fake (grades 5 to 9)

- **Hook:** A rumour is spreading around the school. Where did it come from?
- **What they do:** Follow a story through several "sources": a screenshot, a post, an article, an interview. Judge each one and find the original.
- **Questions:** *Who made this? What do they gain? Can we find a second source? What is missing?*
- **Your move:** After the mission, ask students to apply the same questions to something real, such as a headline they have seen this week.

## Whole-class and school-wide

### 11. Escape Room Review (any subject)

- **Hook:** You are locked in. Every door needs the right answer.
- **What they do:** Move through rooms; each exit stays closed until a question has been answered correctly. Teams race to open the last door.
- **Questions:** The review questions for your unit, one per door.
- **Your move:** Put teams on the clock for fun, or hold the clock and ask them to explain each answer to you.
- **Build it:** Exactly the **gate** pattern in the [walkthrough](/docs/editor/walkthrough/schoolyard/#a-gate-that-needs-a-conversation): an exit hotspot with a condition on a variable that a right answer sets. See [Patterns](/docs/editor/patterns/).

### 12. Welcome to Our School (new students, any age)

- **Hook:** A scavenger hunt through the building.
- **What they do:** Find the office, library, canteen and gym; meet the staff (as characters); collect the information they need on day one.
- **Questions:** *Where do you go if…? Who helps you with…?*
- **Your move:** Run it in the first week as a team-building activity: mixed-grade teams, one tablet each, and a prize for the first to finish *with every question right*.
- **Build it:** A hand-built mission with photographs of your own school as scene backgrounds is a strong start, and a lovely thing for returning students to improve.
