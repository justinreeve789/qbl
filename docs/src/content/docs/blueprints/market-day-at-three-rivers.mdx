Make a mission called Market Day at Three Rivers for grades 3 to 4. Teach and assess C3 economics D2.Eco.1.3-5, D2.Eco.4.3-5 and D2.Eco.5.3-5 and Common Core 3.OA.D.8 and 4.MD.A.2. ```

## Scenario design

<Steps>

1. **Ferry Landing.** On-ramp. Mirembe hands over the clay pot. “The baker has the cake. Go and ask.”
2. **The barter chain.** Three villages, four trades, in an order students must discover by being refused.
3. **Token round.** Same stalls, now with price boards. The counting table and the two-step problems live here.
4. **The frost.** Snow falls in the orchard. Five apples, a new price, and a real choice.
5. **Festival Green.** Payoff. Lanterns or apple pies appear, depending on the team's choice. Build and debrief.

</Steps>

## Puzzles

<div class="bp-puzzles">

- The chain is the puzzle. Each refusal names what that person wants, so students piece the order together.
- Four lanterns at 3 tokens each, paid with 20: how much change? A wrong answer shows the coins laid out and asks which step was missed.
- The counting table rejects a basket that costs 23 tokens and shows by how much.

</div>

<Aside type="tip" title="Optional harder objective">
Find the cheapest way to feed twelve guests using the price boards in all three villages.
</Aside>

## Teacher plan

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Before" icon="comment">
    Ask: “Could we live without money and just swap things?” Most say yes.
  </Card>
  <Card title="During" icon="laptop">
    Expect groans in the barter chain. That is the lesson, so do not hand out the order. Teams that split up finish it faster.
  </Card>
  <Card title="After" icon="approve-check">
    Ask what was annoying and what fixed it. Then: “What did your team give up at the frost, and was it worth it?” The report shows each team's choice.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Example" icon="star">
    Use it to open an economics unit. The words scarcity, trade and opportunity cost go on the wall afterwards, attached to moments students remember.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## Why QuestLab is the best tool here

<BlueprintWhy>

A definition of money as a medium of exchange is only words until a child has been refused four times with a pot in their hands. A live classroom trading game teaches the same thing and is worth running once a year, but it takes a full period to set up, quiet students trade little, and the rules bend. Here every student walks the whole chain, the rules are the same for all, and the relief when tokens arrive is personal. The frost then makes scarcity a decision the student made, with a cost they can name.

</BlueprintWhy>
