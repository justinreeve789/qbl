```text wrap title="Hitchhiker Seeds: generator prompt"
Make a mission called Hitchhiker Seeds for grade 2. Teach and assess NGSS 2-LS2-2, 2-LS2-1 and 2-LS4-1 and Common Core 2.MD.D.10. Students are seed scouts helping Ranger Dalia bring plants back to Burnt Hill after a fire. Scenes: a meadow gate, a riverside, an oak grove, a windy ridge, a potting shed and Burnt Hill. Seeds travel four ways and students collect one of each in any order: a burr that sticks to them when they walk through the meadow grass, a floating pod they fish from the river with a net, a berry seed a bird drops, and a winged seed caught on the ridge. In the potting shed an instrument has four pots with a light switch and a water switch each and a Grow one week button; a meter shows each plant's height. Ask for a prediction first. Wrong ideas to surprise: a plant in a dark cupboard grows fine if it has water; seeds only grow where people plant them. A wall chart sorts the team's seeds into wind, water, fur and food as a picture graph, with how-many-more questions. On Burnt Hill students use seeds on sunny wet patches and shady dry patches, then a short cutscene skips a season to show which grew. Shared goal: twelve patches replanted by the team. Invent a twist where a squirrel takes an acorn, buries it and forgets it. Build scene on the hill: students place plants and label how each seed got there. Debrief: why do plants need their seeds to travel?
```

## Scenario design

<Steps>

1. **Meadow Gate.** On-ramp. Walk through the grass. “Something is stuck to your sock!” The burr goes in the bag before anyone names it.
2. **Riverside, Oak Grove, Windy Ridge.** Open order. Each has one seed to win and one habitat to notice. Birds land, a squirrel climbs, leaves blow.
3. **Potting Shed.** The fair test. One switch at a time. Then the picture graph.
4. **Burnt Hill.** Plant, wait a season, see what came up. The team counter reaches twelve and the hill turns green.
5. **The Squirrel's Oak.** Payoff. A seedling stands where the squirrel dug.
6. **Build and debrief** on the hill.

</Steps>

## Puzzles

<div class="bp-puzzles">

- The floating pod drifts out of reach. Combine a stick and a sieve to make a net.
- The cupboard pot grows tall, thin and yellow, then falls over. Students expected tall to mean healthy.
- A seed used on a shady dry patch does not sprout. Dalia asks what the shed test showed.

</div>

<Aside type="tip" title="Optional harder objective">
Find the fifth way a seed travels, a pod that pops, hidden on the ridge.
</Aside>

## Teacher plan

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Before" icon="comment">
    Hold up an apple. “How could the seeds inside end up growing far from the tree?” List the ideas.
  </Card>
  <Card title="During" icon="laptop">
    Let teams split up to collect; the graph needs all four kinds. Pause before the Grow button and take predictions aloud.
  </Card>
  <Card title="After" icon="approve-check">
    Compare the class list with what they found. The report shows who changed two switches at once, a sign the fair-test idea has not landed.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Example" icon="star">
    Start real beans in a cupboard and on the sill the same day. The mission gives the result now; the beans confirm it in two weeks.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## Why QuestLab is the best tool here

<BlueprintWhy>

Plants take weeks and a class gets one result per pot, so a mistake in the test cannot be redone. Seed dispersal by animals cannot be staged indoors at all. Here the child is the animal: the burr rides on their sock before anyone explains it, which is the model the standard asks for. A video shows popping pods beautifully and makes a good closing clip, but it asks for no prediction. The real bean test and a sock walk through long grass are the right partners, and the plan includes both.

</BlueprintWhy>
