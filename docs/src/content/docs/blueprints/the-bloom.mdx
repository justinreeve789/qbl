```text wrap title="The Bloom: generator prompt"
Make a mission called The Bloom for grades 8 to 10. Teach and assess Common Core HSF-LE.A.1, HSF-LE.A.3 and HSF-IF.B.4 and NGSS HS-LS2-1. Students are lake managers working with Reservoir Manager Ana Folau at Lake Sedra, a 4,096 hectare reservoir. An invasive floating fern has appeared: 1 hectare this week, 2 next week, doubling every week. Each clearing boat removes 20 hectares a week. Scenes: a boat ramp, a ranger station, a planning room, an upstream farm, a fish market and the lake in week 12. At the boat ramp students measure the patch in two different weeks before anyone names the pattern. Ask for a prediction: if the fern covers the whole lake in week 12, in which week is it half covered? In the planning room an instrument called the season planner has a slider for the week removal starts, from 0 to 11, and a slider for boats, from 1 to 10; a graph shows fern area against week and a meter shows percent of the lake covered. The goal is to stay under half cover through week 12. Two wall tables show the boats' running total and the fern's area so students compare equal differences with equal factors. Wrong ideas to surprise: half-way in time means half covered; more boats can always make up for a late start; growth that doubles will double for ever. For the third, the station's records from another lake show growth slowing near the top, and an upstream slider for nutrient runoff from farms raises or lowers that ceiling. People around the lake want different things, a fisher, a farmer and the water company, and none is a villain. Invent a twist where one missed hectare in a side cove brings the fern back after the team thinks it has won. Build scene: students place markers on a lake map and label their plan with the evidence for it. Keep the language at a grade 7 reading level. Debrief: why does starting early matter more than working hard?
```

## Scenario design

<Steps>

1. **Boat Ramp.** On-ramp. Pace out the patch: 1 hectare. A week later: 2. Then 4. Ana asks for a guess at week 12.
2. **Ranger Station.** The half-covered question. Most answers cluster around week 6.
3. **Planning Room.** The planner. Ten boats from week 9 fail. One boat from week 3 wins.
4. **Upstream Farm and Fish Market.** The lake's people. Runoff feeds the fern; the fishers cannot launch.
5. **The ceiling.** Records from a lake that filled. The curve bends. Students move the runoff slider and watch where it levels.
6. **Side Cove.** The twist. One hectare left behind is the whole problem again in twelve weeks.
7. **Week 12.** Payoff. Open water, or a green lake, by the team's plan. Build and debrief.

</Steps>

## Puzzles

<div class="bp-puzzles">

- Half covered in week 11, one week before full. The miss shows the table for weeks 10, 11 and 12.
- The tables: one column grows by adding 20, the other by multiplying by 2. Students name which is which and predict week 8 for each.
- The planner: find the latest start week at which three boats still win.
- The ceiling: at high runoff the fern levels off near the whole lake; at low runoff it stalls far lower.

</div>

## Why QuestLab is the best tool here

<BlueprintWhy>

People misjudge doubling even after being shown the riddle, because knowing the trick is not the same as planning against it. Here students spend a budget, start too late, and lose to the curve. Then they replay it until they can say why. A graphing activity is a strong rival for the pure maths and belongs in the unit. It does not supply the season in two minutes, the people with competing needs, or the ecology that bends the curve. A duckweed jar is real and slow, and shows one curve in three weeks.

</BlueprintWhy>
