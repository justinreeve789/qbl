## Scenario design

<Steps>

1. **Fair Stall.** On-ramp. Yuki mixes a drink on the scale. The number drops as it fizzes. “Did you see that?”
2. **Pantry.** Three powders, three tests. Which jar is the baking soda?
3. **Weigh Station.** Six runs: three changes, lid off and lid on. Sugar water and melted ice do not lose a hundredth. Open fizz loses 1.65 g. Sealed fizz loses nothing.
4. **The loose lid.** The twist. A slow slope on the graph instead of a drop.
5. **Back Kitchen.** The close-up bench: pour, stir, heat, and boil sugar water dry to get the sugar back.
6. **Inspector's Desk.** Make the claim. The stall gets its stamp. Build and debrief.

</Steps>

## Puzzles

<div class="bp-puzzles">

- Powder tests: only one fizzes with vinegar, only one melts and browns in the pan, and the salt crystals are tiny cubes under the magnifier.
- Prediction board: before each run students mark heavier, lighter or the same. The misses stay on the board.
- The balloon: with the lid swapped for a balloon, the weight holds and the balloon swells.
- New or not: sugar water is not new, because the sugar comes back. Fizz is new, because a gas appeared that was in neither jar.

</div>

<Aside type="tip" title="Optional harder objective">
Predict the weight after fizzing with twice the baking soda, lid on, then test it.
</Aside>

## Teacher plan

  <Card title="Before" icon="comment">
    Ask: “If I stir a spoon of sugar into water until I cannot see it, does the cup weigh more, less or the same?”

  </Card>

## Why QuestLab is the best tool here

<BlueprintWhy>

This standard is hard to show honestly in a real classroom. School balances read to the gram, cups leak gas, and spills happen, so the real lab often seems to prove that matter vanished. Here the balance reads to a hundredth and the lid is a switch, so the only difference between losing 1.65 g and losing nothing is whether the gas could leave. A lecture states conservation and a video shows it, and students nod until they watch a real drink go flat. The real fizz is still worth doing for the senses, as the plan says.

</BlueprintWhy>
