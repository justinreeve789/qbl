```text wrap title="Festival Colors: generator prompt"
Make a mission called Festival Colors for grades 6 to 7. Teach and assess Common Core 6.RP.A.1, 6.RP.A.2 and 6.RP.A.3, with 7.RP.A.2 as the optional harder objective. Students are dyers' apprentices working for Master Dyer Farida at a riverside dye works. The invented Lantern Fair needs five banners in one exact colour, fair green, which is 2 cups of blue to 3 cups of yellow per banner. Scenes: a dye yard, a mixing shed, a pigment market, drying lines, a storehouse and the fair gate. In the mixing shed an instrument called the vat has a slider for cups of blue from 0 to 20 and a slider for cups of yellow from 0 to 30; a meter shows how close the mix is to fair green, a readout says too blue or too yellow, and another shows total cups. Students mix one banner's worth first, with no explanation. Then ask for a prediction: to make five banners, what do we do to each colour? The key wrong idea to surprise is adding the same amount to both, for example going from 2 and 3 to 5 and 6; the miss explains that blue more than doubled while yellow only doubled. A second wrong idea: a bigger batch must look darker. On the drying lines a table of banners, blue and yellow fills in as the team succeeds. Ask for the yellow needed for 1 cup of blue. At the market, compare 3 jars for 12 coins with 5 jars for 18 coins. Invent a twist where a barrel leaks and only 9 cups of yellow remain, so students work out the blue to match and how many banners that makes. Optional harder objective: one merchant's price list is proportional and the other's hides a fee; students decide which by table and graph. Build scene at the fair gate: students place a banner and label their own colour recipe with one equal ratio. Debrief: why does adding the same to both change the colour?
```

## Scenario design

<Steps>

1. **Dye Yard.** On-ramp. Farida hands over two blue cups and three yellow. Pour, stir, and the first banner comes out right.
2. **Mixing Shed.** The vat. Predict, then scale. The 5 and 6 pot comes out bluish and stays on the rack as a reminder.
3. **Drying Lines.** The table grows one banner at a time. Unit rate question.
4. **Pigment Market.** Better buy. Bonus: the two price lists.
5. **Storehouse.** The twist. Nine cups of yellow and a deadline.
6. **Fair Gate.** Payoff. Five matching banners rise at dusk with lanterns behind them. Build and debrief.

</Steps>

## Puzzles

<div class="bp-puzzles">

- The vat goal for five banners accepts only 10 and 15. A 5 and 6 mix reads too blue, with the reason.
- A student who doubles to 4 and 6 gets a perfect match at a smaller size, which shows the colour did not get darker.
- With 9 cups of yellow, the matching blue is 6 and the banners are 3. Two more banners need 6 more cups of yellow.
- Better buy: 4 coins a jar against 3.6 coins a jar.

</div>

<Aside type="tip" title="Optional harder objective">
2 kg for 9, 4 kg for 18, 6 kg for 27 is proportional. 2 for 10, 4 for 18, 6 for 26 is not, and the graph misses zero.
</Aside>

## Teacher plan

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Before" icon="comment">
    Pose it cold: “The recipe is 2 blue to 3 yellow. I want more, so I use 5 blue and 6 yellow. Same colour?” Count the yeses.
  </Card>
  <Card title="During" icon="laptop">
    Solo first, so every student commits to their own scaling. Then pair up to explain the rack of failed pots to each other.
  </Card>
  <Card title="After" icon="approve-check">

  </Card>

## Why QuestLab is the best tool here

<BlueprintWhy>

Adding the same amount to both parts is the best-known wrong turn in ratio work, and it feels fair, so being told it is wrong rarely sticks. Here the student's own 5 and 6 pot comes out the wrong green, with a meter to confirm it. Real paint is the natural rival, but 5 to 6 and 2 to 3 are too close for the eye to judge in a paper cup, measuring is rough, and each try costs paint and time. A video can show the right method and never touches the student's own mistake. Here the mistake is made, seen and fixed in under a minute.

</BlueprintWhy>
