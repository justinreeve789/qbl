```text wrap title="Four Skies: generator prompt"
Make a mission called Four Skies for grade 3. Teach and assess NGSS 3-ESS2-1, 3-ESS2-2 and 3-ESS3-1 and Common Core 3.MD.B.3. Students are junior weather scientists working for Chief Forecaster Anahi at a sky station. From the station's balloon dock, four portals lead in any order to four invented places with real climate types: Tamba, a rainforest town with heavy rain; Qasr, a desert outpost that is hot by day and cold at night; Nuvik, a polar base with snow; and Port Ellery, a mild coast with a storm season. Use the weather for each scene. In each place students inspect a painted thermometer and rain gauge and collect two data cards, one for July and one for January, and a local keeper shows a thirty-year log book. Back at the station a data wall shows the team's cards as a table and a scaled bar graph of July rain where one square is 20 mm: Tamba 240, Qasr 0, Nuvik 20, Port Ellery 60. Ask how-many-more questions. Wrong ideas to surprise: one day's weather tells you the climate; deserts are always hot; polar places have no summer. Invent a twist where it rains in the desert on the day of the visit, and students check the log book before deciding what kind of place it is. Final problem: a storm surge is coming to Port Ellery. An instrument has a slider for surge height from 0 to 3 metres and switches for a sea wall, houses on stilts and sandbags; a meter shows how many homes flood. Students test, then choose the claim the evidence supports. Build scene: students place protections in Port Ellery and label why. Debrief: how is climate different from weather?
```

## Scenario design

<Steps>

1. **Sky Station.** On-ramp. Read the station's own thermometer, pick up the card case, step into a balloon.
2. **Tamba, Qasr, Nuvik, Port Ellery.** Any order. Rain pours, heat shimmers, snow blows, wind gusts. Two cards and one log book in each.
3. **Rain in the desert.** The twist, wherever the team visits Qasr. Siham opens thirty years of records.
4. **Data Wall.** The table and graph fill as cards arrive from all teammates.
5. **Surge Room.** The instrument. Free testing, then the claim.
6. **Port Ellery, after the storm.** Payoff. The town stands or is patched up, depending on the design. Build and debrief.

</Steps>

## Puzzles

<div class="bp-puzzles">

- Cards are needed from all four places, so a team that splits up fills the wall four times faster.
- A frozen rain gauge in Nuvik must be thawed with a flask from the station before it can be read.
- On the graph, how much more rain fell in Tamba than in Port Ellery? A wrong answer highlights the two bars and counts squares aloud.
- In the Surge Room, sandbags alone fail at 2 metres. The miss says how many homes flooded and where the water came in.

</div>

<Aside type="tip" title="Optional harder objective">
Find the design that keeps every home dry at 3 metres.
</Aside>

## Why QuestLab is the best tool here

<BlueprintWhy>

A class weather station is worth keeping, but it records one place for a few weeks, and climate is a comparison across places and decades. No class can stand in rain, desert heat and a blizzard in one lesson, or open a thirty-year record in each. A video tours the same climates but hands over the conclusions; here students collect the numbers and build the graph they are asked to read. The surge instrument adds what no model in a tray can: the same storm, repeated exactly, against each design.

</BlueprintWhy>
